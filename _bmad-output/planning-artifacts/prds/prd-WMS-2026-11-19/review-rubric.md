# PRD Quality Review — Zimple Fulfillment Phase 1 (WMS Core)

## Overall verdict
The PRD holds up well: the thesis (JST parity plus the two named JST gaps, the **sorted** state and cancelled-goods hold) is sharp. The glossary is unusually disciplined, most of the 114 FRs carry testable consequences, and assumptions and non-goals are tagged honestly. The risk is that it is not yet build-ready for a launch-grade, 2-month plan. The PRD itself says the scope does not fit (§9.2 NOTE FOR PM), but it offers no provisional priority tiers. Its gating dependency (Zimple OMS, Q-3) has no fallback. A few internal contradictions also sit on the core guardrails: order cancellation in the WMS, and which stock formula is pushed to the OMS. It is fit to feed UX and architecture now. Story sequencing should wait until those four items are fixed.

## Decision-readiness — adequate
Trade-offs are named rather than smoothed over. §1 says outright "Pricing will mirror JST and there is no technical moat". §6.2 frames "No manual stock adjustment exists anywhere" as "deliberate, not a missing feature". Addendum §B records what each decision replaced, including the reversal from JST-as-OMS to Zimple OMS. The Open Questions are genuinely open, and two of them are correctly marked phase-blockers (Q-1, Q-3).

What weakens decision-readiness is that the biggest decision is deferred entirely. The PRD admits "The scope is large for a small team in 2 months. This PRD carries 114 FRs" (§9.2), then hands the cut to Q-1 without proposing one. A decision-maker reading it cannot tell which FRs the thesis treats as non-negotiable. A core guardrail is also contradicted inside the document (see the cancellation finding below).

### Findings
- **[critical]** No provisional prioritization across 114 FRs (§9.1, §9.2 NOTE FOR PM, Q-1). The PRD says its own scope does not fit 2 months, yet every FR carries equal weight. Story creation will treat all 114 as day-one. Q-1 waits on external data (the pilot's real JST usage). The PRD can still rank by its own thesis now: the gap-closers (FR-85–97), the ledger and cutover (FR-42–43, FR-111–114) and the core flows are must-haves; FR-65, FR-75 production-line packing, FR-83, FR-84, FR-110 scheduled reports and FR-23 are candidates to defer. *Fix:* Tag each FR (or each §4.x feature) as P0 / P1 / defer-candidate, marked `[ASSUMPTION]` pending Q-1, so epics can be sequenced before the parity checklist arrives.
- **[high]** The "WMS never cancels orders" guardrail is contradicted (§3 OMS, §6.2, FR-58 vs FR-19, FR-95). The glossary says the WMS "never creates, edits or cancels orders", and FR-58 says "No edit or cancel control exists on orders". Yet FR-19 grants a special permission to "cancel order after work started", and FR-95 has a Supervisor "approve a cancel-after-pick request". Engineers cannot tell whether to build a cancel action, or who originates the request. *Fix:* State whether FR-95 approves an OMS-originated request (and remove the FR-19 permission), or carve out an explicit exception in §6.2.
- **[high]** The gating dependency has no contingency (§7 Zimple OMS, Q-3, addendum §B.2). "Does Zimple OMS exist today?" is a phase-blocker, and SM-1 assumes the pilot leaves JST for both OMS and WMS. Nothing says what happens if Zimple OMS slips. The obvious fallback, the original JST-as-OMS plan, is ruled out only implicitly. *Fix:* Add a `[NOTE FOR PM]` with an explicit fallback (for example, a generic-API connection to JST as an interim OMS) or an explicit "no fallback; Phase 1 slips with OMS" decision.

## Substance over theater — strong
Little furniture here. The Vision is category-specific: 30,000 orders a day, 11.11 spikes, and two named JST gaps ("splits parcels by carrier at handover with no sorted state", "cannot hold a cancelled order's goods"). It would not transfer to another PRD. The JTBD lines each drive features: the CS "under a minute" line, the floor-staff "make mistakes impossible to commit" line behind FR-67/FR-76, and the company "never oversell" line. The UJ protagonists are openly tagged as illustrative. Most NFRs carry product-specific thresholds (500 ms p95 scan feedback, 2 s label print, 5-minute stock push), not adjectives. NFR-7 Security is the closest to boilerplate but still names server-side enforcement and warehouse scoping.

### Findings
- **[medium]** The UX differentiation claim is thinly measured (§1, NFR-9, SM-7). §1 says Phase 1 "must win on... a modern UX that floor staff learn faster". The only metric is SM-7, "≤ 1 [ASSUMPTION], and below JST", and unlike SM-4 it plans no JST baseline measurement. *Fix:* Add "JST onboarding baseline to be measured before cutover" to SM-7, or drop "below JST".

## Strategic coherence — adequate
The thesis is clear and the gap-closing sections are the most fully developed (§4.10 and §4.11 open with "This closes JST's biggest/second gap"). Success Metrics validate the thesis rather than activity: SM-1 cutover, SM-6 daily balance and SM-8 "cancelled before handover that still shipped = 0". Counter-metrics are present and well chosen; SM-C3 "Feature or screen count delivered" directly guards against parity theater. The MVP kind (replacement / problem-solving) is coherent.

Coherence slips in how the parity rule is applied. It is used to cut features (§9.2: "resolved to Later by the JST-parity rule"). But add-ons the mockups mark as optional (FR-65 auto pick lists, FR-83 prep, FR-84 VAS, FR-75 production line, FR-27 kit assembly) are kept "per the brief" without any parity test.

### Findings
- **[medium]** The parity rule is applied one way only (§9.2, FR-65, FR-83, FR-84, Q-5, Q-12). Layout map and temperature log are cut by parity, while add-ons survive on brief authority. Is JST parity the rule or not? *Fix:* Run the add-ons through the same rule, or state why the brief overrides it, and fold the answer into the P0/P1 tagging.
- **[medium]** The CS job has no requirement or metric behind its target (§2.1 CS JTBD). "with evidence... in under a minute" maps loosely to FR-56/57/80, but no FR consequence, UJ or SM carries the one-minute bound. *Fix:* Add a consequence to FR-57, for example "order detail with timeline and video link opens from any search key in ≤ N s", or drop the number.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate
Most FRs meet the bar, with concrete, testable consequences: FR-77 "±5% or 20 g, whichever is larger", FR-90 "Packed today = handed over + cancelled before handover + pending, with no overlap", and FR-42 "A line is written only after the destination is scanned". The gaps cluster in algorithms, dashboards and reports, and in a handful of hedged bounds.

### Findings
- **[high]** The stock-bucket formulas conflict, and the oversell guard is unclear (§3 ATP and Stock section, FR-4, FR-41, FR-52). The glossary has ATP = "Normal stock minus reserved minus blocked lots". FR-4 has available-to-sell = "on-hand − pending orders − damaged/quarantine − returns locations", with no blocked-lot or below-shelf-life exclusion. FR-41 has on-hand = "normal + returns + damaged". Quarantine is named as a deduction, but it is not one of the three stock sections, so it is unclear whether quarantined stock is ever in on-hand. The figure pushed to marketplaces is exactly what the company's "never oversell" JTBD depends on. *Fix:* Define on-hand, ATP and available-to-sell once in the glossary, place quarantine (section or location attribute), and state whether blocked or short-life lots are excluded from what goes to the OMS.
- **[medium]** Suggestion algorithms have no testable outcome (FR-37, FR-79). FR-37 suggests putaway "using room type, the SKU's existing locations and free capacity" but gives no ranking. FR-79 "suggest a box (with backup ranks)" gives no selection criterion. QA cannot write a pass/fail case. *Fix:* State the ranking order (for example, an existing location of the same SKU-lot with capacity, then the nearest empty location in the room type) and the box rule (smallest box whose usable dimensions fit the computed volume and weight).
- **[medium]** Dashboards and reports are lists of nouns (FR-109, FR-110, FR-55). "pack accuracy", "on-time %" and "productivity vs target per stage" are undefined, and so is where the target is set. The eight reports ("inbound analysis", "returns analysis"…) have no content definition in the PRD. *Fix:* Define each metric once (reusing SM definitions where they match), and point each report to its catalogue entry or give its columns.
- **[medium]** Hedged or missing bounds (FR-20, FR-61, FR-62, FR-65, FR-108, FR-16). Examples: "lock the account for about 15 minutes"; "An idle session times out" (no duration); "a countdown (e.g. 10 minutes)"; "for example 300 orders"; "There is a cap on open system lists" (no value, not stated as configurable); "(temporary receipt open, awaiting putaway, stuck move, pending inspection and similar)"; FR-16 has no consequences at all. *Fix:* Replace each with a default value plus "configurable", and close the FR-108 list.
- **[medium]** The write-back for warehouse-created ASNs is unspecified (FR-30 vs FR-3, §3 OMS). The OMS owns ASNs ("ASN lines" read-only, FR-3), but FR-30 lets the WMS create one. Nothing says whether it syncs to the OMS or how its receipt result (FR-39 → FR-4) lands there. *Fix:* Add a consequence stating the sync direction.
- **[low]** Short-shelf-life acceptance uses two mechanisms (FR-36, UJ-1 vs FR-105). FR-36 and UJ-1 call it "supervisor confirmation", while FR-105 lists "short-shelf-life receipt" as an approval type. *Fix:* Pick one; if it is an approval, say so in FR-36.
- **[low]** The FR-89 no-show outcome is ambiguous. "returns the goods to the pickup point" leaves unclear whether the goods go back to stock, to the next appointment or into a hold. *Fix:* Name the destination state.

## Scope honesty — adequate
Tagging discipline is strong. There are 35 inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags and all are indexed in §12. §8 Non-Goals does real work ("Not an OMS", "Not a TMS"). §4.15 openly admits "Most of this section is inferred". Deferred handover extras are named, not dropped silently.

Open-items density is high for a launch-grade PRD: 12 OQs (2 phase-blockers), 35 assumptions and 2 NOTE FOR PM. Most assumptions are sensible defaults rather than gaps. However, the two blockers plus the NFR assumptions (NFR-1 peak multiple, NFR-2 latency) mean the PRD is green-lit for design, not for build. The bigger issue is one large piece of scope hidden in an NFR.

### Findings
- **[high]** Hidden scope multiplier in an NFR (NFR-10, FR-35, FR-44). "A warehouse with no PDAs can run every flow on the web" (also "A warehouse without PDAs can run the whole flow on the web", FR-35) requires a web equivalent of every PDA flow: picking, counting, putaway and sorting. That roughly doubles the floor-flow surface in a plan already over capacity, yet it sits in a device-support bullet, not in §9.1. The pilot is unlikely to need it if it has PDAs (UJ-1–UJ-5 all use PDAs or stations). *Fix:* Move it to §9 as an explicit scope decision; mark it P1 or defer for the pilot unless the pilot lacks PDAs.
- **[low]** NOTE FOR PM callouts are sparse for the number of tensions. There are only 2, while the cancellation guardrail contradiction, the add-on vs parity tension and the web-only fallback are all real tensions with no callout. *Fix:* Add NOTE FOR PM at those points.

## Downstream usability — adequate
This is a chain-top PRD (§0 names bmad-ux, bmad-architecture and epics), so this dimension matters. IDs are clean: FR-1–114, UJ-1–6, NFR-1–11, SM-1–9, SM-C1–3 and Q-1–12 are all contiguous and unique, and every cross-reference spot-checked resolves (FR-6→FR-98, FR-49→FR-105, FR-69→FR-50, FR-28→FR-45, FR-39→FR-4, FR-5→§4.11). Each §4.x carries "Realizes UJ-n". The glossary is strong. The weak spot is the actor vocabulary.

### Findings
- **[medium]** Actor drift against the §4 actor list (line 181). The list names 14 actors, but the FRs use undeclared ones: "Planner" (FR-62, §4.8), "Prep staff" (FR-83), "Counter" (FR-48), "Office user" (FR-20, FR-30), "Customer admin" (FR-23), "Authorized user" / "Staff" / "User" (FR-26, FR-43, FR-53, FR-96). Roles Warehouse Head and Handover Lead (FR-18) also appear. Permission design and story actors will diverge. *Fix:* Map each to a declared actor or add it to the list (and to FR-18 default roles).
- **[low]** Undefined locations and terms. "return staging" (FR-93), "hold location" (FR-59), "return-to-shelf containers" (FR-97), "parcel staging" (FR-32), "on-hand" and "available-to-sell" are not in the glossary. "Carrier pickup rounds" (FR-14) and "carrier wave" are used interchangeably. *Fix:* Add the terms or map them to existing glossary terms (CXL, Quarantine, Carrier wave).

## Shape fit — strong
This is a multi-role B2B operations product with meaningful floor UX and a downstream chain, and the PRD is shaped for that. There are six UJs with named protagonists covering inbound, picking, packing, handover, returns and cutover. The FRs are condensed, with rule-level detail pushed to the addendum and catalogues ("Where the PRD and a catalogue disagree, the PRD wins"), which keeps 114 FRs readable. It is not over-formalized: there are no UJs for admin-only screens, and the shared engines (§4.13) are capability-spec shaped, which fits.

### Findings
- **[low]** There is no UJ for the supervisor or manager role the cut-off thesis rests on. "Know before a wave is missed, not after" (§2.1) is a headline JTBD, but no UJ shows the FR-60 cut-off board in use. *Fix:* Optional. Add a short UJ in which a Supervisor reacts to negative slack, to give UX a protagonist for the board.

## Mechanical notes
- **Assumptions Index roundtrip:** clean. All 35 inline tags map to §12 entries (grouped by section), and no orphan index entries were found.
- **ID continuity:** FR-1–114 have no gaps or duplicates, and the "114 FRs" in §9.2 and Q-1 matches.
- **Q-7 vs FR defaults:** Q-7 says the cancellation-freshness default (30 vs 15 min) and the replenishment interval (60 vs 30 min) are open. FR-92 states "older than 15 minutes" and FR-46 states "default 60" as settled. Add a "(default pending Q-7)" cross-reference.
- **Q-8 vs FR-49:** FR-49 defers thresholds to FR-105, which is fine. Add a Q-8 cross-reference in FR-105's "tiered by pieces, % and value".
- **UJ protagonists:** all 6 are named with inline context. UJ-2 to UJ-5 lack an explicit "Entry state" line, while UJ-1 has one. This is minor.
- **Required sections:** all present for launch-grade stakes (Vision, users, UJs, glossary, FRs, NFRs, constraints, dependencies, non-goals, scope, SMs with counter-metrics, OQs, assumptions).
